01Purpose

About

I am most interested in studying how power is held and reproduced over time, particularly through the organisation of work, and how increasing automation may redistribute it. Difficult problems that reward sustained attention suit me best, and I would rather be challenged than settle into routine.

Since I was eighteen I have worked in street fundraising, hospitality, COVID-19 pathology collection (including hotel quarantine) and, for nearly four years, customer service and administration at a self-storage business. Each of these workplaces organised labour differently, and observing them first-hand has shaped my interest in how work is structured and whom it serves. Time spent travelling, often well away from the usual tourist routes, has shaped my thinking in a similar way, particularly about how differently inequality is lived from place to place. I returned to study in 2025 through a university pathway diploma.

Questions I’m interested in

  • Who holds power, how is it maintained over time, and why do those without it so often accept arrangements that work against them?
  • How would power be distributed in a society whose economy no longer needs most people’s labour?
  • Why do people with shared material interests come to see themselves as opponents, divided by race or income, while the wealthiest go largely unchallenged?

Does technological progress distribute power or concentrate it?

Whether progress distributes or concentrates power depends less on the technology than on the social and institutional frameworks into which it emerges. Drawing on Lukes and Clegg, I trace how platform monopolies and a research agenda tilted towards labour-replacing automation concentrate power, and how policy intervention could distribute its gains more widely.

Major essayDeterring Arrival: Asylum Policy in Australia and the United States

How do Australia and the United States keep asylum seekers from reaching the territory where they could claim protection?

  • Asylum policy
  • Migration
  • Comparative policy

Both countries have constructed similar architectures of deterrence in response to comparable moral panics. Geography explains most differences in form, offshore detention in Australia and land buffers in the United States. The more significant contrast is durability: Australia’s bipartisan consensus differs from US policy, which shifts between administrations and is contested in the courts.

HIS10006 Propaganda: A History of Media and Politics · 2026

Major essayInsulating News from the Market and the State

Should news and information, as public goods, be quarantined from the commercial market, and would that make them independent?

  • Media policy
  • Public goods
  • Public broadcasting

I argue that news is only partly a public good, and that quarantining it from the market would not secure its independence. Cases from the Chicago press in 1918 to Poland’s public broadcaster in 2021 suggest that removing the market leaves control with the state. News should instead be insulated from both, through regulatory protection and secure funding.

SOC20013 Race, Ethnicity and Migration · 2026

Tutorial presentationMigrant Settlement and Gentrification in Footscray

What happens to a migrant gateway suburb when urban renewal and gentrification arrive?

  • Migration
  • Housing
  • Urban renewal
  • Melbourne

Footscray has served as a gateway suburb for successive waves of migrants, but government-led renewal and rents rising faster than in the outer west are eroding that role; the Ethiopian and Somali communities now live mostly in Wyndham, Brimbank and Hume. I recommend affordable housing beyond key workers, secure leases for migrant traders and settlement services where new arrivals now live.

Why do three major accounts of the Cold War, written before, shortly after and a decade after the Soviet collapse, reach different conclusions?

  • Cold War
  • International relations
  • Historiography

The period in which Hobsbawm, Gaddis and Jervis each wrote, the information available to them and their theoretical frameworks all shaped their accounts. They largely agree on the observable features of the conflict, diverge on what drove the superpower rivalry, and converge, by different routes, on the Soviet role in ending it.

Much of my academic writing is comparative, whether across historians’ accounts of the Cold War, national approaches to asylum, or competing positions on how news should be funded. The most significant development in my writing this year followed tutor feedback to use sources as evidence, theory or context rather than as authority for claims already made. Building systems with AI

Although I have no formal training in programming, I have found that most of the work of directing AI lies in specification. A task must be divided into components, each with an explicit definition of completion expressed as conditions that can be verified as true or false, and the system is then refined, detail by detail, until those conditions hold. Since June 2026 I have applied this approach as a contract developer on an AI agent platform, where I have also learnt that a tool’s design largely determines whether people use more than its most basic functions.

  • Specification and acceptance criteria
  • Systems design
  • Interface design
  • Iterative testing

Reflection: the Not Our Game campaign

In developing the Not Our Game campaign strategy, I learnt most from its messaging. The most striking available figure was the 40.7% rise in domestic assaults on Origin nights, yet the research advised against leading with it. Publicising the prevalence of a harmful behaviour can normalise it, and a campaign that treats every fan as part of the problem risks alienating the audience it most needs to reach. The fan-facing material therefore begins from a view most men already hold, that disrespect is unacceptable, and suggests practical ways to act on it.

The experience has made me more attentive to how framing shapes the reception of an argument, independently of its accuracy. As the strategy was developed in a group, my sections also needed to align with the work of other members. In future research and writing, I intend to test my assumptions about framing against the available evidence.
