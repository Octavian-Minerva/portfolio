01Purpose

About

I want to research how wealth, work and technology shape who gets ahead, so that access to opportunity is not decided by the family you are born into. I am drawn to research because I like hard problems, and I would rather be challenged than stuck in routine.

Since I was eighteen I have worked a string of jobs that taught me more about how work is organised than I expected: a month of street fundraising, two years at a burger chain, swabbing people at COVID testing sites and in hotel quarantine, and nearly four years behind the counter at a self-storage business. Travelling on a budget, including on the cheapest trains in India, did the same for how I see inequality. I came back to study through a diploma in 2025, and those experiences shape most of what I write about.

Questions I’m interested in

  • How do we stop access to opportunity being decided by the family you are born into?
  • Why does rising inequality so often set people against each other, rather than against those accumulating wealth and influence?
  • What will the wider rollout of AI do to the labour market, and who will gain from it?

Now, November 2026

  • Halfway through my BA, majoring in Politics, Power and Technology
  • Moving into infrastructure work on an AI agent platform
  • Reading into scientific management and how work is organised

SOC20013 Race, Ethnicity and Migration · 2026

Major essayDeterring Arrival: Asylum Policy in Australia and the United States

How do Australia and the United States keep asylum seekers from reaching the territory where they could claim protection?

  • Asylum policy
  • Migration
  • Comparative policy

I argue that both countries have built similar architectures of deterrence on the back of comparable moral panics. Geography explains most differences in form, offshore detention against land buffers. The sharper contrast is durability: Australia’s bipartisan consensus against US policy that swings between administrations and the courts.

HIS10006 Propaganda: A History of Media and Politics · 2026

Major essayInsulating News from the Market and the State

Should news and information, as public goods, be quarantined from the commercial market, and would that make them independent?

  • Media policy
  • Public goods
  • Public broadcasting

I argue that news is only partly a public good, and that quarantine would not make it independent; from the Chicago press in 1918 to Poland in 2021, removing the market simply leaves control with the state. News should instead be insulated from both, through regulatory protection and secure funding.

SOC20013 Race, Ethnicity and Migration · 2026

Tutorial presentationFootscray: Migrant Settlement and Urban Renewal

What happens to a gateway suburb for migrants when urban renewal and rising rents arrive?

  • Migration
  • Housing
  • Urban renewal
  • Melbourne

Footscray has welcomed successive waves of migrants, but state-led renewal and rising rents are pricing out that role, and the gateway is moving west to Brimbank and Wyndham. I recommend public housing that goes beyond essential workers, and long-term leases for the African traders being priced out of their shopfronts.

HIS10003 Global History · 2026

Term essayThree vantage points of the Cold War: Hobsbawm, Gaddis, and Jervis

Why do three serious accounts of the Cold War, written before, just after and a decade after the Soviet collapse, disagree?

  • Cold War
  • International relations
  • Historiography

I compare Hobsbawm, Gaddis and Jervis and argue that when each wrote, what they could know and the frameworks they used shaped their accounts. They agree on what was observable, split on what drove the rivalry, and converge, by different routes, on the Soviet role in ending it.

ART20003 Skills and Strategies for Social Change · 2026

Group campaign strategyNot Our Game

How can State of Origin broadcasts help prevent domestic and family violence without treating fans as the problem?

  • Social campaign
  • Domestic and family violence
  • Messaging

Not Our Game is a campaign strategy to prevent domestic and family violence on State of Origin nights, when recorded domestic assaults in New South Wales rise by 40.7%. It asks the NRL and its broadcasters to carry prevention messaging and support-service information during the 2027 Origin broadcasts. I produced the campaign website and wrote the report’s messaging and artefact sections.

03ART20003 and beyond

Skills audit and reflection

In ART20003 I learnt how to plan and deliver a social campaign, working with a group to take Not Our Game from a problem to a strategy with a clear request. My part was the messaging, and it showed me how much evidence should drive a strategy. Research on social norms suggests that publicising how common a harmful behaviour is can reinforce it, so our fan material does not lead with assault statistics, however striking they are.

That is the same skill my essays this year have pushed hardest: structuring an argument so the evidence does the work. Outside uni, building systems with AI has sharpened how I break a problem into parts and check the result, and pushing these tools since the first ChatGPT release has kept me learning.

As I finish my degree and keep working, I want to bring that habit of letting evidence lead into research on inequality and work.
